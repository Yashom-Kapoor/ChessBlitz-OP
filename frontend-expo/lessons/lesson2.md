# Hanging Pieces: Spotting Free Material

## Introduction

In chess, a **hanging piece** is a piece that can be captured because it is undefended or not protected enough by other pieces.

Hanging pieces are one of the most common mistakes in chess. Learning to recognize them helps you win material, avoid blunders, and improve your chess skills.

Before making a move, ask yourself:

**“What can my opponent capture after I move?”**

Today, we're going to practice spotting hanging pieces and learn how to use them to win material.

## Puzzle 1: Spot the Hanging Piece

**Difficulty:** Beginner   
**[Open Puzzle 1 on Lichess](https://lichess.org/1sV2Hr22#35)**

### What to Look For

Start by scanning the board for pieces that are not protected by another piece. Pay special attention to valuable pieces, such as queens, rooks, and bishops.

Ask yourself these questions:

- Which of my opponent's pieces are under attack?
- Is any valuable piece undefended?
- Can I capture a piece without losing one in return?
- Are any of my own pieces hanging?

### Your Goal

Find the piece that can be captured safely. Look for an opportunity to win material without giving your opponent an equal or better trade.

### Key Takeaway

Always check whether your opponent has a valuable piece that you can capture for free. A quick scan of the board can reveal opportunities that are easy to miss.

## Puzzle 2: Hanging Pieces and Tactics

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**[Open Puzzle 2 on Lichess](https://lichess.org/tzeeBEc2#115)**

### What to Look For

Sometimes, a hanging piece is not immediately obvious. You may need to look for a tactical move that creates an opportunity to win material.

Try using this checklist:

1. **Look for forcing moves.** Checks, captures, and threats can limit your opponent's responses.
2. **Check the defenders.** Determine which pieces protect the target.
3. **Look ahead.** Imagine your opponent's most likely response after each candidate move.
4. **Recheck the board.** A piece that is protected now might become undefended after a move.

### Your Goal

Find the tactical sequence that allows you to take advantage of a vulnerable piece. Do not stop after finding a promising move; consider how your opponent can respond.

### Key Takeaway

A hanging piece can be the result of a tactical sequence. Look beyond the immediate move and consider how the position changes after both players respond.

## Puzzle 3: Removing the Defender

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**[Open Puzzle 3 on Lichess](https://lichess.org/vaqz2bx6#37)**

### What to Look For

In harder positions, a valuable piece may appear well protected. However, you might be able to win it by attacking or removing the piece that defends it.

This idea is called **removing the defender**.

To find this tactic:

1. Identify a valuable enemy piece.
2. Determine which piece is defending it.
3. Look for a capture, threat, or combination that removes or distracts the defender.
4. Calculate what happens once the defender can no longer protect the target.

Be careful: removing a defender does not always mean capturing it immediately. Sometimes you must first create a threat or force your opponent to respond.

### Your Goal

Find the combination that takes advantage of the defensive relationship between your opponent's pieces. Calculate the full sequence before committing to your move.

### Key Takeaway

A piece may be protected now but become vulnerable after a tactical exchange. Look for ways to eliminate, distract, or overload its defender.

## Summary: The Hanging Piece Checklist

Before every move, take a few seconds to review the position.

- **Scan:** Are any enemy pieces undefended?
- **Attack:** Can I capture a piece safely?
- **Protect:** Is one of my own pieces hanging?
- **Calculate:** What is my opponent's best response?
- **Recheck:** Will my move leave another piece undefended?

The most important habit is to **check what your opponent can capture after every move**. Practicing this consistently will help you avoid blunders and recognize more opportunities to win material.

## Quick Quiz

Test what you learned. Choose the best answer for each question.

### Question 1

You notice that an enemy bishop is attacked by your knight and is not protected by any other piece. What should you check first?

- A. Whether your king is in check
- B. Whether you can win more pawns elsewhere
- C. Whether you should castle
- D. Whether the bishop can be captured safely

**Correct Answer:** D

**Explanation:** If the bishop is undefended and can be captured without a tactical drawback, you may be able to win material for free. Always calculate your opponent's possible response first.

### Question 2

Your opponent moves their queen to a square where your rook can capture it, and the queen is not protected. What is the main mistake?

- A. The queen is overextended
- B. The queen is pinned
- C. The queen is hanging
- D. The queen is trapped

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** A hanging piece can be captured because it is undefended or insufficiently protected. If the queen can be taken safely, you may win a significant amount of material.

### Question 3

What is the best habit for avoiding hanging pieces?

- A. Always trade pieces whenever possible
- B. Only look for checks and captures
- C. Check what your opponent could capture after your move
- D. Move the same piece several times in a row

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Before committing to a move, check which of your pieces might be left undefended. This simple habit can prevent many blunders.


## Lesson Summary

When you're playing a game, use this quick checklist before making a move:

### 1. Look for Free Pieces

Can I capture an enemy piece without losing something important?

### 2. Check My Opponent's Threats

After I make my move, what can my opponent capture?

### 3. Find the Defenders

If a piece looks like it can be captured, ask what is protecting it.

### 4. Look for Ways to Remove Defenders

Sometimes the best tactic is not to capture the target immediately, but to first get rid of its defender.

### The Golden Rule

> **Before you make a move, look at what your opponent can capture next.**

The more you practice this, the more quickly you'll start noticing hanging pieces during your games.
