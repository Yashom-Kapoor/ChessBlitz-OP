# Using Pins

## **Introduction**

A **pin** is a chess tactic where a piece is restricted from moving because something valuable is behind it on the same rank, file, or diagonal.

Imagine your bishop attacks an enemy knight, but the enemy king is directly behind that knight. If the knight moves away, it exposes the king to check, so the knight cannot legally move off that line.

Pins are powerful because they can make enemy pieces much less useful, even before you capture anything.

Today, we're going to learn how to recognize pins, understand why pinned pieces cannot always escape, and use pins to win material.


## **Two types of pins**

1. **Absolute pin**

A piece is pinned to its king. It cannot legally move if doing so would expose the king to check.

Think: the pinned piece is legally stuck.

2. **Relative pin**

A piece is pinned to a more valuable piece, such as a queen. It can legally move, but doing so may allow the opponent to capture that valuable piece.

Think: the pinned piece can move, but there may be a big cost.

**Before making a move, ask yourself:**

> "If this piece moves, what is hiding behind it?"

The key idea is that the enemy queen cannot always escape an attack just because it has plenty of available squares.

When a queen is pinned to its king, moving it off the attacking line would expose the king to check. That makes the move illegal.

This is what makes an **absolute pin** so powerful: the pinned piece cannot simply move out of danger.

Instead of looking only at the piece being attacked, look at the entire line behind it. The piece in front may be protecting something far more important than itself.

A useful habit is:

> When you attack a piece, check whether its king is directly behind it.

## **Puzzle 2: Use the Pin to Win Material**

Now that you know how a pin works, it's time to use one to gain an advantage.

[https://lichess.org/training/199Tm](https://lichess.org/training/199Tm)

This time, your goal isn't just to identify a pinned piece. You need to figure out how to exploit its restricted movement to win material.

Before choosing your move, ask yourself:

- Which enemy piece is pinned?
- What is the pinned piece protecting?
- Can you add another attacker to the pinned piece?
- Is there a capture that becomes possible because the pinned piece cannot move safely?

Try the puzzle and calculate the consequences of your move before continuing.

---

The important lesson is that creating a pin is only the beginning — you need to exploit it.

When a piece is pinned, its ability to defend other pieces or escape an attack may be limited. You can take advantage of this by increasing the pressure on the pinned piece or targeting something it was supposed to protect.

For example, imagine a knight is pinned to its queen. If you attack the knight with another piece, your opponent may struggle to save it without giving up the queen.

This is how a pin can turn a seemingly ordinary position into an opportunity to win material.

Remember this sequence:

**Find the pin → Increase the pressure → Win material**

One important detail: a **relative pin** does not make moving illegal. Your opponent can still move the pinned piece, but doing so may cost them something more valuable. Always calculate the resulting position instead of assuming the piece must stay still.


## **Lesson Summary**

When you're playing a game, use this checklist to spot and exploit pins.

### **1. Look for pieces on the same line**

Check ranks, files, and diagonals for an enemy piece with a king or valuable piece behind it.

### **2. Identify the type of pin**

- **Absolute pin:** The piece cannot legally move if doing so exposes its king to check.
- **Relative pin:** The piece can move, but doing so may lose a more valuable piece.

### **3. Find what the pinned piece is defending**

A pinned piece may be unable to escape an attack or safely perform its defensive job.

### **4. Increase the pressure**

Look for opportunities to attack the pinned piece again or capture something it can no longer safely protect.

### **The Golden Rule**

> Don't just spot the pin. Ask yourself what the pin allows you to win.

The more you practice recognizing these patterns, the easier it becomes to turn restricted enemy pieces into winning opportunities.
